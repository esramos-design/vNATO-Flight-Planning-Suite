1. Introduction & Regulatory Context

Operational Air Traffic (OAT) flight planning governs military and state-configured aircraft operations, requiring strict adherence to national Aeronautical Information Publication (AIP) standards and virtual network (such as VATSIM) filing rules. Because live pre-file systems automatically reject tactical flight rules (T), flights must utilize a safe fallback framework (I for IFR or Z for hybrid rules) while preserving OAT routing rules inside the main route string and Item 18 remarks.

2. Country-Specific Routing & Remark Standards

  • France (DGAC / DIRCAM): The route string must never start with the term "OAT". However, active OAT phases must be declared by placing RMK/OAT at the beginning of the Item 18 remarks field.
  • Germany (BMVg / Luftwaffe): The route string must start with "OAT" followed by a space. Remarks do not require the OAT prefix (using standard tags like RMK/VIRTUALNATO.ORG), and airspace blocks follow structured formats (e.g., EDR208 308 1600 1700 FL100 FL350 FOR TRAINING) alongside standard fuel endurance parameters (FUEL0200).
  • United Kingdom (RAF / MilAIP): The route string must start with "OAT" followed by a space. Operations incorporate specialized national infrastructure including Radar Corridors (e.g., LICHFIELD RC, DAVENTRY RC), Low-Flying Areas (e.g., LFA7), and specific STAYINFO tags.

3. Route Construction & Formatting Rules

  • No Airfield ICAOs in Route: Never insert departure or destination airfield ICAO codes inside the middle of a route string.
  • Space-Separated Syntax: Chronological waypoints, radar corridors, and transition descriptors must be separated by single spaces.
  • STAY Segment Sequence: Low-Flying Areas, Air Refueling tracks (AAR), or working blocks require a STAYX/HHMM entry attached to their waypoint in the route, paired with matching STAYINFOx/ remarks.

4. Tactical Flight Scenarios & Examples

OAT IFR — Ferry / Liaison Flight & Radar Corridors

Route Example: CGY LICHFIELD RC LFA7 STAY1/0015 DCT CGY

OAT VFR — Low-Level Navigation & Low-Flying Areas (LFA)

Route Example: CGY/MID07S LICHFIELD RC LFA7 STAY1/0020 DCT LCH RC DCT CGY

Remarks Example: STAYINFO1/LFA7 LOWAT DELAY 0020

Air Refueling (AAR) & Danger Areas

Route Example: BZN DCT DAVENTRY RC DCT AARA8 STAY1/0030 DCT DAVENTRY RC DCT BZN

Remarks Example: STAYINFO1/AARAS AAR MARSA/NATO11/ DELAY 0030 1200 TO 1230

Detailed Parameter Glossary

Route & General Parameters

  • Jurisdiction / Country Standard: Selects national OAT reporting and remark conventions for France (DGAC/DIRCAM), Germany (BMVg/Luftwaffe), or United Kingdom (RAF/MilAIP).
  • Generator Mode: Switches between building individual SWIFT Route strings, Item 18 Remarks, or the complete standalone vPILOT flight plan string.
  • Date of Flight (DOF): Flight departure date formatted in standard 6-digit YYMMDD format.
  • Flight Rules: I (IFR), V (VFR), T (OAT Tactical), Y (IFR then VFR), Z (VFR then IFR). Note: VATSIM parser compatibility automatically maps tactical rules safely.
  • Type of Flight: General operations category indicator (M for Military, S for Scheduled, N for Non-scheduled, G for General Aviation, X for Special).
  • Speed & Altitude Units: Cruising speed units (N knots, M Mach, K km/h) paired with numerical values, and Flight Level/Altitude/Metric units (F, A, S).
  • Route Type & Transfer Points: Pre-configured OAT templates, GAT-to-OAT, OAT-to-GAT, and VFR transition waypoints.
  • STAY Segments: STAY1, STAY2, STAY3 integrated with chronological route fixes and duration in HHMM format.

Item 18 & Remarks Parameters

  • Wake Turbulence Category: Aircraft wake turbulence classification based on maximum certificated takeoff mass (J Super, H Heavy, M Medium, L Light).
  • Equipment & Transponder: Field 10a radio communication, navigation, and approach aid equipment codes (e.g., SDE3G, SDT) and Field 10b surveillance equipment codes (e.g., S, E).
  • PBN / Performance Based Navigation: Multi-selected RNAV/RNP capabilities concatenated without spaces (e.g., S1).
  • NAV Capability: Additional navigation equipment identifiers or equipment augmentation (e.g., TACAN, RNAV5).
  • STS / Special Handling: Required ATC special handling indicators such as STATE, NONRVSM, MARSA, or SAR.
  • SEL / SELCAL: Selective calling radio system code format (e.g., ABDE).
  • SUR / Surveillance: Surveillance equipment capabilities and data link identifiers (e.g., 260B).
  • PER / Performance: Aircraft performance data, PBN capabilities, or RNP compliance indicators (e.g., C1).
  • ORGN / Origin: Originator address or dispatch identifier for the filed flight plan (e.g., LFBOOANX).
  • COM / Comms: Communication capabilities and voice frequencies.
  • REG / Registration: Aircraft registration or military tail number identifier (e.g., FA1801).
  • OPR / Operator: Operator designator or military unit identifier (e.g., FAF, GAF, 29 SQN ROYAL AIR FORCE).
  • Fuel Endurance & EET: Total fuel endurance (e.g., FUEL0200) and estimated elapsed times (EET/).
  • STAYINFO / Working Area Notes: Detailed military exercise working area descriptions mapped automatically to STAYINFO1/, STAYINFO2/, and STAYINFO3/.
  • RMK / VSO Tag: Regulatory tags like RMK/OAT, organization signatures like VIRTUALNATO.ORG, and optional student tags like VSO TRAINEE.

Code Surveillance & Transponder Capabilities

3. Performance-Based Navigation (PBN) Specifications

PBN qualifiers define authorized RNAV and RNP navigation standards. Multiple selected capabilities are concatenated directly into Item 18 without spaces (e.g., PBN/B2S1).

  • RNAV Specifications (Enroute & Terminal):
    • A1: RNAV 10 (RNP 10) oceanic/remote operations.
    • B1 - B5: RNAV 5 operations utilizing all sensors, GNSS, DME/DME, VOR/DME, or INS/IRS.
    • C1 - C4: RNAV 2 precision operations.
    • D1 - D4: RNAV 1 high-density terminal operations.
  • RNP Specifications (Advanced Performance):
    • L1: RNP 4 oceanic/remote monitoring.
    • O1 - O4: RNP 1 Basic area navigation and radius-to-fix capability.
    • S1: RNP APCH (Standard approach capability).
    • S2: RNP APCH with BARO-NAV vertical guidance.
    • T1 / T2: RNP Authorization Required (AR) APCH with or without RF turns.

METAR Decoding & Reading Guide

Meteorological Aerodrome Reports (METAR) follow a strict sequential format governed by international WMO/ICAO standards. Use this operational reference guide and descriptor tables to decode weather observations rapidly during flight planning.

Anatomy of a Standard METAR String

LFBO 141730Z 24015G25KT 9999 -RA FEW025 SCT042CB 18/12 Q1013 NOSIG

  1. Station Identifier: 4-letter ICAO code indicating the reporting aerodrome (e.g., LFBO).
  2. Date & Time Stamp: Day of the month and Zulu time in hours/minutes followed by 'Z' (e.g., 141730Z).
  3. Wind Vector: True wind direction in degrees and speed in knots, including gust peaks if applicable (e.g., 24015G25KT). Variable ranges indicated with 'V' (e.g., 210V270).
  4. Visibility: Prevailing horizontal visibility in meters (e.g., 9999 for 10km or more). Statute miles or fractions may appear in North American reports (e.g., 1 1/2SM).
  5. Runway Visual Range (RVR): Touchdown zone visibility along active runways (e.g., R27L/1400FT).
  6. Weather Phenomena: Combinations of intensity, descriptors, and phenomena types (e.g., -RA light rain, TSRA thunderstorm with rain).
  7. Sky Condition / Cloud Cover: Cloud coverage layer and base height in hundreds of feet AGL (e.g., FEW025 few at 2,500ft, SCT042CB scattered cumulonimbus at 4,200ft).
  8. Temperature / Dew Point: Ambient air temperature and dew point in degrees Celsius separated by a slash (e.g., 18/12). Negative values prefixed with M.
  9. Altimeter Setting: Atmospheric pressure report. QNH millibars prefixed with Q in Europe (e.g., Q1013) or inches of mercury with A in North America (e.g., A2992).
  10. Recent Weather & Trends: Optional supplemental remarks or landing forecasts (e.g., NOSIG - no significant change expected).

Terminal Aerodrome Forecast (TAF) Decoding Guide

Terminal Aerodrome Forecasts (TAF) provide expected meteorological conditions at an aerodrome over a specific 24- or 30-hour validity period. Use this operational guide to interpret forecast trends, operational probability groups, and conditional weather shifts.

Anatomy of a Standard TAF String

TAF AMD LFBO 141730Z 1418/1524 24015G25KT 9999 BKN020
TEMPO 1420/1424 4000 TSRA OVC010CB
BECMG 1506/1508 26010KT CAVOK

  1. TAF Header / Modifier: Indicates report type. Routine TAF or AMD (Amended forecast) or COR (Corrected).
  2. Station Identifier & Issue Time: 4-letter ICAO code and date/time of issuance in Zulu (e.g., LFBO 141730Z).
  3. Validity Period: Valid 2-digit date, starting hour, and ending hour of the forecast window (e.g., 1418/1524 = 14th day at 18:00 UTC through 15th day at 24:00 UTC).
  4. Base Forecast Weather: Prevailing wind, horizontal visibility, weather phenomena, and cloud layers expected across the primary validity block.
  5. Forecast Change Groups: Trend indicators detailing expected shifts over time (detailed below).

TAF Change Indicators & Probability Groups

Indicator Significance / Operational Meaning
BECMG (Becoming) Gradual transition of weather conditions occurring at a regular or irregular rate over the specified time window (e.g., BECMG 1506/1508). Permanent change once window concludes.
TEMPO (Temporary) Temporary fluctuations expected to last for less than one hour per occurrence and accumulate to less than half of the indicated time period. Conditions revert to base forecast afterward.
PROB30 / PROB40 Indicates a 30% or 40% probability of occurrence for thunderstorms, heavy precipitation, or low visibility hazards during the stated sub-window.
FM (From) Rapid, complete, and permanent sweep of prevailing conditions starting at an exact hour and minute (e.g., FM151230). Supersedes all previous groups.

Specialized Aerodrome Terms

CAVOK: Visibility 10km+, no cloud below 5,000ft or MSA, no CB/TCU, and no significant weather.
NSW: No Significant Weather expected to affect approach or departures.

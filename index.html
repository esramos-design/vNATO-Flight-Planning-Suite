1. Introduction & Operational Context

This manual assists pilots in accurately filing Fields 8 (Route) and 11 (Remarks) for military OAT flights adapted from national AIP standards for virtual network operations.

📄 Open Official Selected Country PDF Manual
Active Country-Specific Formatting Rules & Differences:
• France (DGAC / DIRCAM): The Route field must never start with the word "OAT". However, the OAT status must appear at the beginning of the remarks (RKS) field to indicate that at least one OAT phase is taking place.
• Germany (BMVg / Luftwaffe): The Route field must start with "OAT" followed by a space. Remarks do not start with "OAT" (uses standard RMK/VIRTUALNATO.ORG and optional VSO TRAINEE). STAYINFO airspace blocks follow a strict structured format (e.g., EDR208 308 1600 1700 FL100 FL350 FOR TRAINING), and fuel endurance (e.g., FUEL0200) is standard.
• United Kingdom (RAF / MilAIP): The Route field must start with the term "OAT" followed by a space. Utilizes national infrastructure such as Radar Corridors (e.g., LICHFIELD RC, DAVENTRY RC), Low-Flying Areas (LFA7), and specific STAYINFO formats (e.g., STAYINFO1/LFA7 LOWAT DELAY 0020).

2. Instructions: How to Build Your Route & Remarks

  • No Airfield ICAOs in Route: Do not include departure or destination airfield ICAO codes inside the middle of your route string.
  • Space Separation: Separate chronological route waypoints, radar corridors, and performance descriptors with single spaces.
  • STAY Segment Sequence: Low-Flying Areas, AAR, or working areas require a STAYx/HHMM entry in the Route, with matching details in STAYINFOx/.

5. Detailed Parameter Glossary

Route Generator Parameters

  • DOF: Flight departure date in YYMMDD format.
  • Flight Rules: I (IFR), V (VFR), T (OAT Tactical), Y (IFR then VFR), Z (VFR to IFR).
  • Speed & Altitude Units: Knots/Mach/Kmh (N, M, K) and Flight Level/Altitude/Metric (F, A, S).
  • STAY Segments: STAY1, STAY2, STAY3 with duration in HHMM.

Remarks Generator Parameters

  • Type of Flight: Indicates the general category of operations being conducted. Common military and operational codes include M (Military), S (Scheduled air transport), N (Non-scheduled air transport), G (General aviation), and X (Other / Special operations).
  • Wake Turbulence Category: Denotes the aircraft's wake turbulence generation classification based on its maximum certificated takeoff mass. Standard ICAO codes include J (Super - for A380), H (Heavy - 136 kg or more), M (Medium - between 7 kg and 136 kg), and L (Light - 7 kg or less).
  • Equipment & Transponder: Field 10a (e.g., SDE3G) and Field 10b surveillance codes covering full capabilities from N (No transponder), A, C, E, H, I, L, P, S, to X.
  • PBN / Performance Based Navigation: Multi-selected RNAV/RNP capabilities concatenated without spaces (e.g., S1B1).
  • STS / Special Handling: Identifiers such as STATE, NONRVSM, MARSA, SAR, MEDEVAC, FLTCK, HAZMAT, HEAD, and HUM.
  • SEL / SELCAL: Selective calling radio system code.
  • SUR / Surveillance: Surveillance capabilities and transponder data link codes.
  • PER / Performance: Aircraft performance data, PBN capabilities, or RNP compliance indicators.
  • ORGN / Origin: Originator address or dispatch identifier for the filed flight plan.
  • COM / Comms: Communication radio capabilities and frequencies.
  • REG/ & OPR/: Aircraft registration and operator identification (e.g., 29 SQN ROYAL AIR FORCE, GAF, FAF).
  • FUEL / EET / STAYINFO: Fuel endurance (e.g., FUEL0200), estimated elapsed times, and specific area working notes.
  • RMK/ & VSO Tag: Regulatory tags like RMK/OAT FERRY, VIRTUALNATO.ORG, plus optional VSO TRAINEE student tag. For France, OAT appears at the beginning of remarks.

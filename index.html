V1.1 notes / limitations
  • Coordinate fixes such as 454901N0013507E are decoded without navdata.
  • Named fixes are resolved locally from imported X-Plane-style Waypoints.txt and Navaids.txt data. ZIP import is supported in modern Chromium browsers.

  • Live position comes from the public VATSIM Data API and is polled about every 15 seconds. Crossing time is interpolated between consecutive network positions.

Recommended Workflow

  1. Select jurisdiction and generator mode. Confirm the country convention before mission-specific data is entered.
  2. Enter aircraft and dispatch data. Callsign, type, departure, EOBT, arrival, EET and alternate.
  3. Configure route semantics. Route, transfer points, STAY blocks and speed/level changes must be associated with the intended waypoint.
  4. Complete equipment and Item 18. Do not advertise capability the simulated aircraft does not have.
  5. Generate and run preflight. Correct structural errors before importing.
  6. Verify the VATSIM form. Compare the imported form with the generated FPL; importer limitations are covered in VATSIM Compat.

Position Report Logic

ACTUAL crossing

When live tracking detects the aircraft crossing the active fix, the suite records an interpolated UTC crossing time from successive VATSIM position samples.

ESTIMATED next fix

The next-fix ETA is calculated from current progress. It remains an estimate and can change as groundspeed/track evolves.

Why Hasn't My Fix Advanced?

  • Unresolved fix: sequential protection can intentionally prevent the tracker from skipping an unresolved operational point.
  • Callsign mismatch: the VATSIM callsign must match the connected pilot exactly.
  • Not connected / stale feed: verify the live status and aircraft data fields.
  • Route geometry: confirm the fix resolved to the intended coordinate, especially with duplicate identifiers.
  • Off-route flight: the aircraft may not pass sufficiently close to the active fix for a crossing to be confirmed.

V5.6 COUNTRY-AWARE GUIDE

OAT / Military Operations

Country-specific planning guidance for France, Germany and the United Kingdom, separated from common ICAO/iOAT concepts so national procedures are not silently merged.

OAT
Simulator use only. The material below is an operational training summary derived from the supplied vNATO / EUROCONTROL reference documents and adapted for the suite. It is not a replacement for current real-world AIP/MilAIP publications.
Source-integrity rule: when two supplied documents differ, V5.6 shows the difference instead of silently choosing one. Country guidance is therefore version-labelled.

Common Planning Architecture

MISSION→COUNTRY→FLIGHT RULES→ITEM 15→STAY / TRANSFERS→ITEM 18→VATSIM CHECK

OAT I

Instrument OAT / controlled military routing. Use country guidance for route composition and transition points.

OAT V

Visual OAT / low-level operations. Significant turns, speed/altitude changes and working areas remain explicit in the route.

OAT Tactical

Mission phases that cannot be executed simply as OAT I or OAT V. France explicitly documents OAT T and RTBA as tactical.

Mixed GAT/OAT

Use GAT/OAT transition keywords at the actual change point. Y/Z describes the relevant flight-rule sequence where applicable.

STAY / STAYINFO

STAYx/HHMM belongs in Item 15; the corresponding STAYINFOx/ carries the activity details in Item 18.

Special Handling

STS, PBN, NAV, DOF, REG, OPR, PER, ORGN, COM and RMK remain structured Item 18 data. Do not advertise capabilities not simulated.

SOURCE: Country Introduction v1.7 — 15 APR 2026 · France LOA v1.0 — 01 MAR 2022

The Country Introduction states the module is mandatory for pilots assigned to or wishing to fly in France; minimum OP qualification is required.

Mission Scope

High/medium military routes, low-level, TRA/CBA tactical activity, AAR, AWACS and other missions agreed with French vACC.

METAR Decoding & Reading Guide

Meteorological Aerodrome Reports (METAR) follow a strict sequential format governed by international WMO/ICAO standards. Use this operational reference guide and descriptor tables to decode weather observations rapidly during flight planning.

Anatomy of a Standard METAR String

LFBO 141730Z 24015G25KT 9999 -RA FEW025 SCT042CB 18/12 Q1013 NOSIG

  1. Station Identifier: 4-letter ICAO code indicating the reporting aerodrome (e.g., LFBO).
  2. Date & Time Stamp: Day of the month and Zulu time in hours/minutes followed by 'Z' (e.g., 141730Z).
  3. Wind Vector: True wind direction in degrees and speed in knots, including gust peaks if applicable (e.g., 24015G25KT). Variable ranges indicated with 'V' (e.g., 210V270).
  4. Visibility: Prevailing horizontal visibility in meters (e.g., 9999 for 10km or more). Statute miles or fractions may appear in North American reports (e.g., 1 1/2SM).
  5. Runway Visual Range (RVR): Touchdown zone visibility along active runways (e.g., R27L/1400FT).
  6. Weather Phenomena: Combinations of intensity, descriptors, and phenomena types (e.g., -RA light rain, TSRA thunderstorm with rain).
  7. Sky Condition / Cloud Cover: Cloud coverage layer and base height in hundreds of feet AGL (e.g., FEW025 few at 2,500ft, SCT042CB scattered cumulonimbus at 4,200ft).
  8. Temperature / Dew Point: Ambient air temperature and dew point in degrees Celsius separated by a slash (e.g., 18/12). Negative values prefixed with M.
  9. Altimeter Setting: Atmospheric pressure report. QNH millibars prefixed with Q in Europe (e.g., Q1013) or inches of mercury with A in North America (e.g., A2992).
  10. Recent Weather & Trends: Optional supplemental remarks or landing forecasts (e.g., NOSIG - no significant change expected).

- / + Intensity Light (-) or Heavy (+) intensity modifier (moderate has no sign).

Terminal Aerodrome Forecast (TAF) Decoding Guide

Terminal Aerodrome Forecasts (TAF) provide expected meteorological conditions at an aerodrome over a specific 24- or 30-hour validity period.

Anatomy of a Standard TAF String

TAF LFBO 141700Z 1418/1518 24015G25KT 9999 -RA FEW025 SCT042CB BECMG 1502/1504 20010KT

  1. Report Type: TAF (routine forecast) or TAF AMD (amended forecast).
  2. Station Identifier: 4-letter ICAO aerodrome designator (e.g., LFBO).
  3. Issuance Time Stamp: Day of the month and Zulu time when the forecast was generated (e.g., 141700Z).
  4. Validity Period: Start day and hours through end day and hours in UTC (e.g., 1418/1518 means valid from the 18th hour of the 14th to the 18th hour of the 15th).
  5. Forecast Wind: Mean predicted wind direction in degrees true and speed in knots, including gusts (e.g., 24015G25KT).
  6. Forecast Visibility: Prevailing horizontal visibility in meters (e.g., 9999 for 10km or greater).
  7. Weather Phenomena: Expected precipitation or obscurations (e.g., -RA light rain).
  8. Cloud Layers: Forecast cloud coverage and base heights (e.g., FEW025 SCT042CB).
  9. Change Groups: Subsequent expected weather transitions indicated by timing and trend codes (e.g., BECMG, TEMPO, FM).

TAF Change & Trend Indicators

Code Indicator Type Operational Meaning
FM From Rapid and permanent change occurring at a specific exact hour/minute (e.g., FM142030). Erases all previous conditions.
BECMG Becoming Gradual transition of conditions taking place evenly over a specified time window (e.g., BECMG 1502/1504).
TEMPO Temporary Temporary fluctuations expected to last for less than an hour at a time and covering less than half the period.
PROB30 / PROB40 Probability 30% or 40% probability of occurrence for specific thunderstorm or weather phenomena.
